A rollout is useful when a team can finish a real job with less confusion. Start by naming that job—not by selecting every module in a product catalogue.

## Choose a complete operational problem

Examples include knowing what stock is available, moving confirmed orders through the warehouse, or reviewing courier collections. Pick a problem with an identifiable owner, a clear starting event and a result the team can verify.

Keep the dependencies in view. An inventory workflow may need product identifiers, warehouse locations and opening quantities. A COD workflow also needs the relevant orders and reported collections.

## Prepare the data that determines the result

Review product and variant identifiers, customer and order references, stock by location, provider configuration and the meaning of each status. Keep original exports so imports can be compared with a known starting point.

Do not substitute a plausible number for a missing fact. Unknown stock, money or history needs an explicit resolution before it is treated as operational data.

## Agree what acceptance means

Write down a few representative scenarios. Include the normal path, incomplete data, a rejected action and a retry after an uncertain response. Confirm which person is allowed to approve each consequential step.

A completed screen is not the same thing as a completed operation. The relevant record, quantity or payment must reflect an acknowledged result.

## Expand after the first workflow works

Keep the first scope small enough to verify, but complete enough to be useful. Once the team understands its responsibilities and recovery paths, review the next connected workflow.
